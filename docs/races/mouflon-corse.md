# Corsican mouflon ram — visual specification for a side-view illustration

**Animal:** Corsican mouflon (*mouflon de Corse*). Corsican names: *mufre* (plural, as in the OFB monograph title *E Mufre di Corsica*); EN Wikipedia gives male "Mufro", female "Mufra".
**Taxon:** *Ovis gmelini musimon* var. *corsicana* (the name used by OFB, the PNA and TAXREF). Older or alternative names include *Ovis aries musimon*, *Ovis orientalis musimon* and *Ovis musimon*.
**What to draw:** an adult ram (*bélier*) of the Cinto/Asco population, about 8 years old, in mid-winter coat (December–February), seen in strict side view and facing right.

Each statement carries a tag:
- **[S]** comes from a written source, listed in section 10.
- **[P]** was measured or observed on official photographs of Corsican mouflons (OFB monograph, PNA, ANCGG; see "Visual references" in section 10).
- **[E]** is my own estimate or inference. Treat it as a guide rather than a fact.

Units used in the ratios:
- **H** is the height at the withers.
- **HL** is the head length, from the poll (back of the skull) to the tip of the nose.

---

## 0. The 30-second summary

The animal is a small, compact wild sheep with smooth hair (no fleece). It stands on slim legs that are white from the knees and hocks down. In winter the ram's front half (chest, throat ruff and shoulders) is almost black, and the hindquarters and upper neck are chestnut. A white patch, the **saddle**, sits high on the side just behind the shoulder. A black band runs along the lower flank above a white belly. The rump, muzzle and eye ring are white, and the tail is very short and dark.

The horns are heavy, ridged and roughly triangular in cross-section. They rise from above the eye, sweep up, back, down and forward close to the head, and complete about three quarters to seven eighths of a circle (270–320°). In Cinto rams the tips point toward the throat and neck. The tips never flare outward, and they do not curl up toward the eye as they do in many continental European rams.

## 1. Identity: which individual to depict, and why

**Population.** Corsica has two native populations about 100 km apart, which have been genetically separate for centuries [S PNA, ANCGG]:
- **Cinto / Asco** in the north.
- **Bavella** in the south.

A third nucleus was created at Cagna in 2020 [S OFB]. Mainland "mouflons méditerranéens" are a different case: they are hybridised, and their horns and coats differ [S PNA, OFB, ANCGG].

**Recommended model: an adult Cinto ram aged about 8 years (7–10), in winter coat.** The reasons are:
- **The horns are complete.** They grow very fast until the 3rd year and slow sharply after that. Growth is "practically finished around 7–8 years". From the 8th year it only compensates for wear and broken tips [S PNA, ANCGG]. The shape that is typical of each population is "well distinguishable after the 5th year and accentuates with age" [S OFB Fig. 50].
- **Adult males are always horned** [S PNA, INPN, ANCGG].
- **The winter coat is the most recognisable one.** It shows the white saddle, the black throat ruff (*jabot*) and the strongest dark/chestnut contrast. The saddle and the jabot "exist only in the male and are especially marked in winter" [S PNA]. In summer "the saddle and jabot disappear" [S INPN, ANCGG].
- **The Cinto type is the textbook type.** The saddle is "almost systematic at all ages" at Asco/Cinto and less frequent at Bavella [S INPN]. Cinto males have homogeneous colours [S OFB]. Bavella rams often carry grey hairs, a grey-mixed jabot and, with age, invasive grey patches [S PNA, OFB]. A painter could misread those as hybrid or aberrant animals.
- **The mid-winter body condition is documented.** Cinto males of 7 years and older weigh 36.9 kg on average in mid-winter, after the rut [S PNA Table 1].

**Variants you may want later:**
- **Bavella ram.** The horns are wider and pass above the withers (see section 6). The coat often has grey speckling.
- **Summer ram.** See section 4.3.
- **Ewe.** See the end of section 4.3 (coat) and section 6.6 (horns).

**Do not draw these signs of hybridisation with domestic sheep** [S ANCGG, OFB]:
- An atypical coat: nearly black, light brown or isabelline (pale cream).
- Pure-white piebald patches (*panachures*); these point rather to inbreeding.
- Abnormal horns: too splayed, "banana" shaped, ingrowing toward the jaw or cheek, or short "macaron" coils.

On the leg/chest ratio: young rams look leggy, with long limbs and an underdeveloped rib cage. Old rams look deeper and "closer to the ground" [S ANCGG]. For the 8-year-old, use legs about equal to chest depth.

### 2.3 Build and silhouette

- **Overall body.** "Stocky but of elegant bearing" (*trapu mais d'allure élégante*) [S INPN]. "Males larger and stockier than females" [S ANCGG].
- **Compared with a domestic sheep,** the legs and neck are proportionally longer [S Zoo Barcelona].
- **Adult ram.** Thick neck, deep chest and a convex nose. These traits strengthen with age [S PNA, OFB, ANCGG].
- **Croup.** Short and rounded. Seen from the side, the back of the thigh shows a white edge (the rump patch).
- **Scrotum.** It is "very apparent and voluminous" [S ANCGG] and hangs low, about 30–35 cm above the ground [S Bavarian wildlife portal, citing Piegert/Uloth]. In a faithful profile it shows between the hind legs and is covered in pale hair [P].

## 3. Coat: structure and texture

**Hair, not wool.** The outer coat is short, smooth and lies flat:
- "Smooth hairy coat" / *glattes Haarkleid* [S EN/DE Wikipedia].
- *Kurzhaariges Fell* (short-haired coat) [S Spektrum].
- "Made of hair and not of wool" (said of wild sheep generally) [S Larousse].

Italian Wikipedia calls the hair *ispido* (bristly). Combined with the above, read it as coarse to the touch but lying sleek.

**Winter woolly underfur.** In winter a dense woolly underfur (*bourre laineuse*) grows under the hair [S ANCGG, Toronto Zoo, MITECO, neobiota]. It is never visible as a fleece. It only makes the winter outline fuller and slightly rough, especially on the neck and shoulders.

**Length by season.** In wild sheep the coat ranges from about **10 mm after the spring moult to about 50 mm in autumn** [S Ryder 1976, citing his mouflon study]. Primitive sheep shed large areas at once in a marked spring moult [S Ryder]. A late-spring ram can therefore look patchy (old dark coat beside new short coat). Avoid that look for the reference image.

**Where the hair is longer (winter ram):**
- **Jabot (throat ruff).** Long black hairs from the throat down the front of the neck to the chest [S PNA, INPN, ANCGG, OFB]. Toronto Zoo describes it as a "fringe of long hair down the front of the neck". In profile it hangs as a shaggy black fringe from just behind the chin to between the forelegs. Its hairs are about 8–15 cm long at the longest [E from photos and the ANCGG drawing]. In summer it disappears [S INPN].
- **Neck crest.** Slightly longer, slightly mane-like hair. Males have "a black mane and long hair on the neck and chest" [S neobiota, Watatunga, MITECO].
- **Front of the forelegs.** Rams have long hair on the front of the forelegs [S neobiota, MITECO]. This adds a slightly ruffled dark edge on the front of the forearm [P].
- **Back of the thighs and rump patch.** Slightly longer and fluffier hair [P].

**How the hair lies:**
- On the body it is flat and directed backward.
- On the legs, face and muzzle it is very short and tight.
- Hair whorls are not visible at painting scale.
- In winter the topline and neck show a soft, slightly broken outline. In summer the silhouette is crisp and sleek.

## 4. Colours

### 4.1 How the hex values were obtained

I sampled median pixel colours of each body region on three sets of photos:
- The official OFB and PNA photos of Cinto and Bavella rams (PNA Fig. 2; OFB monograph Fig. 50–51).
- One warm-light photo in the ANCGG article.

Each photo was white-balanced on its own white areas (saddle or muzzle). I then rounded the results to **local colour under neutral, overcast daylight**:
- **Base** is the mid-tone of the region.
- **Shadow** is the colour of the region in its own form shadow.
- **Light** is the colour of the region in direct sun.

Real animals vary, and bright sun pushes the chestnut toward orange (the ANCGG sunset photo reads orange-copper). Treat the values as ±10% in lightness.

**Where the dark flank band runs.** It is "a black longitudinal ventral band which extends on the side from the sternum to the abdominal region, more or less marked according to the individual" [S PNA]. It "does not continue onto the shoulders or the thighs" (stated for females) [S OFB].

In the ram [P]:
- It starts at the elbow, where it is continuous with the black chest.
- It runs straight back along the **lower third** of the trunk to the flank fold in front of the stifle.
- Its upper edge is fairly sharp against the brown side, and its lower edge sharp against the white belly.
- It is about **1/5–1/6 of the chest depth** wide, slightly broader at the front.

**Saddle: shape, position and size** [P, on four ram photos]:
- **Position.** On the **side** of the upper flank, not on the spine. It sits just behind the shoulder blade, over the middle ribs. Its centre is at about **40–55 % of the trunk length from the point of the shoulder**.
- **Vertical extent.** Its top edge lies between the topline and about 20 % of the trunk depth below it, and it never crosses the spine. Its lower edge reaches about **mid-flank** (45–55 % of the chest depth).
- **Shape.** An irregular vertical oval or rounded rectangle, 1.3–1.8 × taller than wide, with ragged and feathered edges. Sometimes it is narrower at the bottom.
- **Size.** Roughly the size of the head: width ≈ 0.18–0.25 × trunk length, height ≈ 0.35–0.45 × chest depth.
- **Variation.** Size varies a lot between individuals. Some Cinto males have only a small greyish blaze [P OFB Fig. 51].

**Front of the legs:**
- All the lower parts are white "except for a narrow black band along the front legs" [S OFB].
- "The limbs also show a white band on the posterior surface, above the knees and hocks" [S OFB].
- So the forearm is black or dark brown on its front face down to the knee, and white on its back face. The black continues as a narrow line or patch over the front of the knee and sometimes partway down the cannon. Below that the leg is white to the hoof [P].
- On the hind leg, the front of the gaskin and hock is dark brown, and the leg is white below the hock [P].

### 4.3 Summer coat (for reference)

- **General colour.** The coat lightens to "a generally light-fawn" colour (*fauve-clair*) in Cinto males [S OFB]. Other descriptions: brown-red shades, lighter or darker [S ANCGG]; "fox red-brown" [S EN/DE Wikipedia]; "rusty brown" [S Stara Zagora Zoo].
  - Body: base `#A5713F`, shadow `#6E4626`, light `#C99A68` (light fawn to about `#B88A5A`).
- **Forequarters.** No longer black: warm brown `#7A5136`. The jabot disappears [S INPN, ANCGG].
- **Saddle.** It **disappears** according to the Corsica-specific sources [S INPN, ANCGG, PNA "surtout marqués l'hiver"]. **Sources disagree here:** EN/DE Wikipedia say summer rams are "fox red-brown … usually with a whitish saddle patch". Follow the Corsican sources and show at most a faint pale area (`#C8B39A`).
- **Hair.** Very short (about 1 cm) and sleek. The silhouette looks slimmer, and the neck much thinner without the ruff [S Ryder; P].
- **Unchanged.** The whites stay (muzzle, eye ring, belly, rump, stockings). The black flank band and the dark front of the forelegs stay but contrast less (`#3A2A20`).

**Ewe, for completeness:**
- Winter: beige-brown to brown `#6E5644`. Summer: light fawn `#B08559`.
- No true saddle or jabot; at most "the start of a saddle" [S PNA, ANCGG, INPN].
- The facial mask "ends up invasive with age" [S PNA].

## 5. Head

**Profile.**
- Adult ram: **convex** nasal line (*chanfrein busqué*), which becomes "frankly Roman-nosed in the oldest" [S PNA, OFB, ANCGG].
- Young rams: "short, wedge-shaped head" [S ANCGG].
- For an 8-year-old: a moderate, continuous convex arc from the forehead to the nose tip. Do not draw a domestic-ram hook [E].
- The forehead between the horn bases is broad and nearly flat [P].
- The back of the skull carries a fibrous "occipital bump" (*bosse occipitale*), hidden inside the horn coil [S ANCGG drawing].

**Muzzle.**
- Narrow and tapering, with a rounded tip and an upper lip that slightly overhangs the lower one [P].
- The **mouth corner** lies about halfway between eye and nose tip, slightly nearer the nose [P].
- The lips are thin and mobile, covered in white hair.
- The **upper lip is split by a philtrum groove** that continues onto the small hairless nose pad (*planum nasale*, divided by fine grooves in small ruminants) [S DocCheck "Äußere Nase"].

**Nostrils.** "Narrow and slightly S-shaped" (*schmal und leicht S-förmig geschwungen*) [S DocCheck]. They sit at the very front of the muzzle and slant obliquely, opening forward and outward. In profile they read as a short curved dark slit just above the upper-lip line [P].

**Eye.**
- Large and set **laterally and high**, just below and in front of the horn base [S DE Wikipedia "seitlich stehende Augen"; P].
- Its centre lies about **1/3 of HL from the poll** (roughly 35–40 %) [P].
- The visible opening is almond-shaped, about 1/9–1/10 of HL long [P].
- The lid rim is dark.
- A pale eye ring surrounds it (section 4).

**Pupil.**
- **Horizontal**, elongated transverse oval to rounded rectangle. Ruminant pupils are a "longish transverse oval" [S DocCheck "Iris"; Larousse "pupilles rectangulaires et horizontales"].
- Dark pigmented knobs (*granula iridica*) sit on the pupil margin and are "larger and more numerous on the upper margin" [S DocCheck]. They show as a slightly lumpy upper edge to the pupil.
- When the head goes down to graze, the eyeball rotates so that the pupil **stays horizontal**; grazers' eyes can rotate 50° or more [S Banks et al. 2015, via EurekAlert]. This matters for animation.

**Iris.** Amber / golden-brown to hazel [P only]. Bright light makes it look pale yellowish, almost whitish on high-key photos (OFB Fig. 50 #4), and shade makes it brown.

**Eyelashes.** No source mentions them. At painting scale, render them as a fine dark upper-lid line [E].

**Preorbital gland.** Mouflon have preorbital, interdigital and inguinal glands [S IT Wikipedia]. In wild sheep there is "a preorbital gland at the inner corner of the eye" [S Larousse]. Draw a small dark slit or pocket just in front of the inner eye corner [E for exact look].

**Ears.**
- "Short" [S PNA], pointed-oval (lanceolate), about 0.3–0.35 × HL [P].
- **Placement relative to the horn.** The ear base is tucked **immediately below and slightly behind the horn base**, at the side of the poll. In profile the ear sticks out sideways or backward from under the first (basal) part of the horn, into the open space inside the coil [P OFB Fig. 50 #4 and #5; ANCGG photo].
- **Carriage.** Held roughly horizontal, often turned forward toward a stimulus. It is never long or droopy [P].
- **Colour.** Outside brown/greyish; the edges and part of the inside are white [S PNA, OFB].

**Facial markings for the 8-year Cinto ram** [S PNA, ANCGG; P]:
- A white muzzle and chin.
- White extending up the front of the nasal bridge to roughly halfway between the nose tip and the eye, fading into grey-brown above.
- A pale eye ring.
- A grey-brown forehead and cheeks.
- The throat below the jaw turning black into the jabot.

The white mask "grows with age, rising toward the eyes and forehead" [S ANCGG]. Its size varies a lot between individuals and is a poor guide to age [S PNA, Garel et al. 2005].

## 6. Horns

### 6.1 Structure and growth

- **Structure.** A bony core covered by a keratin sheath. The horn is permanent and grows continuously from the base. Each winter growth stops and leaves an **annual growth ring** (*cerne*) [S PNA, OFB].
- **Growth rate.** Lambs' horns become visible 10–15 days after birth. Growth is "very rapid until the 3rd year", then slows strongly. After the 8th year it only compensates for wear or broken tips [S PNA, OFB]. Growth is "practically finished around 7–8 years" [S ANCGG].
- **Length by age.** Continental alpine data, for orientation only [S Tyrol hunting authority]:

  | Age | Horn length |
  |---|---|
  | 2 years | ≈ 50 cm |
  | 3 years | ≈ 60 cm |
  | 4 years | ≈ 67 cm |
  | 5 years | ≈ 70 cm |
  | 6 years | ≈ 75 cm |
  | 7+ years | ≈ 78 cm |

  Corsican horns are shorter: Cinto males of 7+ years average 65.3 cm [S PNA].
- **Approximate curl** [E, from length vs coil geometry and the photos]:
  - Yearling: short horns sweeping back.
  - 2 years: about half a circle, tips below the ear.
  - 3 years: about 0.6 of a circle.
  - 4–5 years: about 0.7.
  - **7–10 years: about 0.75–0.9 of a circle (270–320°).**

### 6.2 Geometry in strict profile (ram facing right)

Picture a clock face drawn on the side of the head, with 12 o'clock up and 3 o'clock toward the nose. Its **centre** is the centre of the horn coil, which lies **about 0.5 HL behind the eye and level with or slightly above it**, over the ear base and back of the skull [P OFB Fig. 50 #4; S ANCGG drawing]. Because the coil centre is behind the eye, the horn base sits at the **front** of the coil.

1. **Base (about 2–3 o'clock).** The horn emerges from the top of the skull **directly above and slightly behind the eye**. Its front edge sits just above and behind the orbit, and the ear base is right behind and below it. The two bases nearly meet at the midline. From the side you see only the near one, as a thick root rising from the crown. In a 3/4 view the far horn's arch appears in front of it; do not mistake that for the near horn [P; S ANCGG drawing].
2. **First stretch (2–3 → 12 o'clock).** The horn rises steeply **up and back**. These are the most recent annual segments: short, crowded and the thickest part.
3. **Over the top (12 → 9 o'clock).** It arches **backward** over the poll, well above the ear. The top of the arch is the highest point of the head silhouette, about 0.6–0.8 HL above the eye (more when the head is raised) [P].
4. **Down the back (9 → 7 o'clock).** It descends **behind the skull**, over the top of the neck. This is the rearmost point of the coil.
5. **Under and forward (7 → 6 → 5 o'clock).** It curves **down and forward** below the ear and behind the angle of the jaw. The lowest point of the coil is at about the level of the mouth and chin [P OFB Fig. 50 #4; ANCGG drawing].
6. **Tip (about 5 → 4 o'clock).** The tip runs **forward**, below and behind the cheek, toward the throat.
   - **Cinto rams:** the tips "point with time toward the neck, even the nose/face", and can become **ingrowing** (*rentrantes*). They then touch the body and **rub the hair off at the contact point**. From the front the tips touch "the point of the shoulder", and in profile "the tips are aimed at the shoulder, which they abrade" [S OFB Fig. 50, PNA].
   - **Total sweep:** about 270–320° of arc for a 7–10-year ram. I measured ≈ 270–290° on the Cinto ram photo (tip at about 5 o'clock) and ≈ 318° on the ANCGG "10th-year" drawing (tip at about 4 o'clock) [P].
7. **Lateral component.** The coil also spirals outward from the head. Seen in profile, you can see the ear, the upper neck and the rufous neck hair through the open centre of the coil. The coil is not stuck flat against the head [P].
8. **The ear inside the coil.** In strict profile the short ear points up and back into the open centre of the coil, just behind the ascending base [P OFB Fig. 50 #4].

**Cinto versus Bavella** [S OFB, PNA]:
- **Cinto.** The coil axis is "more vertical" and the horns grow "more parallel". Tips reach the neck or shoulder. Supra-cervical (above-the-neck) horns are rare. **This is the recommended model.**
- **Bavella** (from 6–7 years). Most rams have **heteronymous** horns (the sheath twists on its axis) or **flat** horns (*plan*, "sickle-shaped", curving within one plane). The coil is larger and the **tips pass above the withers** (supra-cervical). From the front the spread can be very wide, and from behind the horns "pass clearly above the back line". The Bavella horn type is closer to the Cyprus and Armenian mouflon pattern [S OFB].

**Types never recorded in native Corsican populations** [S OFB, PNA]:
- **Homonymous** (divergent) horns, whose tips swing away from the body. This contradicts Britannica's general "tips turn outward"; Toronto Zoo also says they "do not flare out at the end".
- The continental pattern where the coil runs "parallel" and narrow and the **tips aim at the nasal bridge or the eye**.

**Size and shape relative to the head:**
- **Coil outer size ≈ 1.0–1.2 × HL wide and ≈ 1.2 × HL tall.** Cinto coils are slightly upright ovals, consistent with OFB's "more vertical axis" [P; S OFB].
- **Profile width ≈ 0.2–0.25 × HL** over the first two thirds of the length, about ¼ HL at the base. It then tapers over the last third to a blunt point, about 0.1 HL just before the tip [P; S Serbian trophy data: mean circumference 23.4 cm at the base, 22.3 cm in the 2nd third, 17.7 cm in the 3rd third].

### 6.3 Cross-section

- "Thick, ovoid at the base … with **three sides**, a **marked fronto-nuchal edge** and an **almost completely rounded fronto-orbital edge**" [S PNA, OFB].
- "Triangular at the base" [S INPN].
- "Ovoid section, thickened on the anterior face" [S ANCGG].

For the painter:
- Draw a **rounded triangle**, with the broad face facing forward and outward.
- The fronto-nuchal edge forms a fairly crisp keel. It runs along the upper, outer rim of the coil and catches a highlight line.
- The other corner is rounded.
- Toward the tip the section becomes oval, then round [E].

### 6.4 Surface

There are two kinds of rings [S ANCGG; drawing on p. 55]:
- **Ornamental ridges** (*anneaux de parure*). Dense, fine transverse ridges a few mm apart. They wrap the whole horn like corrugation and are most pronounced on the front and outer face [P].
- **Annual growth rings** (*anneaux de croissance*). Deeper grooves spaced at irregular intervals that divide the horn into yearly segments.

**Segment spacing, as shown in the ANCGG drawing of a ram in its 10th year:**
- Segment 1, at the tip, is the first-year growth. It is short and often worn or broken off.
- **Segment 2 is the longest** (about a quarter of the coil), then segment 3.
- From year 4 on, each segment is shorter.
- The last 3–4 segments near the base are only a few centimetres each and crowded together.
- On old rams the last rings can form **bulges** (*bourrelets*) [S ANCGG].
- For an 8-year ram, draw about **7 annual rings**.

**Tips.** Old rams wear or rub off their horn tips because the tips can restrict the field of view [S EN/DE Wikipedia]. In mountains, horns rarely exceed 80 cm because of wear [S Tyrol]. The tip is therefore blunt, smoother and paler, sometimes slightly splintered.

### 6.5 Colour of the horn

- **Base to tip.** Darker greyish olive-brown at the base (`#5E4C3C`). Greyish tan along the coil (`#85705A`). Pale, worn, bone-grey-tan at the tip (`#BFAE95`) [P].
- **Surface texture.** Ridge crests catch light (`#A88F73`); grooves and annual rings are darker (`#3E3329`).
- **Habitat.** Horn colour reflects habitat: **dark in forest-dwelling animals, light in rock-dwelling ones** [S OFB]. A Cinto rock-dwelling ram can be on the lighter, more "blond" side. In such horns the growth rings show as darker lines [S ANCGG].

### 6.6 Ewes (not to be drawn, for completeness)

- **How often horned.** Usually hornless or with small horns. The figures disagree:
  - 13 % horned at Cinto vs 43 % at Bavella [S PNA, OFB; Sanchis 2018].
  - About 10 % vs about 75 % [S older INPN sheet].
  - ">50 % at Bavella–Caroux" [S ANCGG].
  - Almost 0 % in Sardinia and Cyprus [S PNA].
- **Size and shape.** 3–20 cm long, thin, cylindrical or flattened, slightly curved backward and often asymmetric. They are set wider apart at the base [S ANCGG, PNA, MITECO, DE Wikipedia]. Bavella ewes may reach 15–20 cm [S PNA].

## 7. Legs and hooves

- **Build.** Slim, dry legs with well-defined knees and hocks, slender cannons and short, fairly upright pasterns [P]. The legs are proportionally longer than a domestic sheep's [S Zoo Barcelona].
- **Forelegs.**
  - Black or very dark front of the forearm, edged with slightly longer hair in winter rams [S neobiota, MITECO; P].
  - White back of the forearm [S OFB].
  - A narrow black line or patch over the front of the knee and sometimes down the cannon [S OFB "narrow black band along the front legs"; P].
  - White below the knee to the coronet [S PNA, ANCGG].
- **Hind legs.**
  - Chestnut thigh, white back edge (rump patch) and white inner thigh [S, P].
  - Dark front of the gaskin and hock [P].
  - White band on the back of the leg above the hock [S OFB].
  - White below the hock [S].
- **Hooves.** "Small hooves without an interdigital membrane" [S INPN]. They are dark slate-black, pointed, and neat and narrow in profile [P].
  - In wild sheep the hoof has a hard outer rim and a softer, spongier sole that grips rock [S Larousse].
  - Interdigital glands are present [S IT Wikipedia, Universalis] but invisible.
- **Dewclaws.** Present (the Bavarian wildlife-agency report mentions damaged *Afterklauen*). They are two small dark horny knobs at the back of each fetlock, just above the hoof [S Bavarian portal; P].

## 8. Behaviour notes for animation

**Activity.**
- Mostly diurnal, with a bimodal or crepuscular rhythm: feeding bouts in the morning and evening, and resting and ruminating in between [S PNA, ANCGG].
- In summer they also feed at night [S Stara Zagora Zoo, Barcelona].

**Social structure.**
- Gregarious, with "fission–fusion" groups. Outside the rut, adult males and females live apart [S PNA].
- Ewe groups are matriarchal (February–October) and led by an old ewe [S ANCGG, Spektrum, waldwissen].
- Males form ram groups from about their 3rd year [S waldwissen].
- Animals move **in single file** along regular trails (*Gänsemarsch*, "in a long line") [S Bavarian portal, Hamburg Wildgehege].

**Vigilance.**
- They have excellent eyesight: they recognise a person at up to 1 km [S DE/EN Wikipedia, waldwissen].
- The eyes are lateral and give a very wide view. Binocular vision covers about 60° [S DE Wikipedia].
- When something moves in the side field of view, the animal **turns its head toward it**, then tests the wind and listens [S DE Wikipedia].
- Pose [P/E]: body freezes, neck rises close to vertical, muzzle level or slightly raised to scent the air, ears swivel forward.

**Alarm.**
- **A brief, repeated hiss or whistle through the nostrils** (*chuintement*, *zischende Pfeiftöne*), mainly from lead ewes. It is often accompanied by **stamping a foreleg** [S ANCGG, DE Wikipedia, waldwissen].
- The hiss gets louder as danger approaches [S DE Wikipedia].
- If the lead ewe warns, the group flees at once. If another animal warns, the group goes on alert and waits for the leader [S DE Wikipedia].

**Flight.**
- They flee toward **inaccessible rock faces** in mountain habitat; in lowland they only manage short sprints [S DE Wikipedia].
- They flee in a compact line along known routes [S Bavarian portal].
- Gait sequence [E]: walk → trot → bounding gallop, with sure-footed short leaps from rock to rock and quick changes of direction.
- For scale only, Larousse gives these figures for the larger **bighorn**: walking stride about 50 cm, gallop about 50 km/h, leaps over 4 m. A mouflon would be somewhat smaller and slower; there is no mouflon-specific figure.

**Grazing.**
- They graze mainly on grasses but browse too [S PNA].
- Head down, forelegs straight, slow steps. The pupils stay horizontal because the eye rotates [S Banks et al.].

**Rut.**
- **Timing in Corsica.** End of October to early January [S PNA]. The peak is mid-November at Bavella and the first half of December at Asco/Cinto [S INPN].
- **Other timings quoted elsewhere** (not Corsica-specific): October/November [S Wikipedia], November–December [S waldwissen], late September/early October [S Britannica].
- **Movement and groups.** From October rams join the ewes in mixed groups. They are not territorial and travel widely between groups [S ANCGG, INPN, PNA].
- **Courtship.**
  - Mouflon-specific: rams **drive oestrous ewes while making rattling and snarling sounds** (*röchelnde und schnarrende Laute*) [S DE Wikipedia].
  - Courtship elements recorded in the domestic ram, the same genus, so expect similar ones: sniffing the ewe, **nudging with the head, foreleg kick, vocalising, flehmen (lip curl), mounting** [S Patel et al. 2005]. Their exact form in the mouflon is not described in the sources I found.
- **Fights.**
  - "The two rivals, **after taking several metres of run-up, strike each other violently, head low**." The crack of the horns "can be heard at great distance" [S ANCGG].
  - Rams "back away from each other, then charge and meet head-on" [S Toronto Zoo].
  - The impacts are audible from far away [S DE Wikipedia, Zoo Barcelona].
  - Mouflon also fight **shoulder to shoulder** in highly ritualised bouts, and **shake the head nervously as a threat** [S IT Wikipedia].
  - Rams fight mostly but not only during the rut [S DE Wikipedia].
  - Fights usually pair rams of similar strength [S ANCGG].
  - The winner may then receive an appeasement gesture: the loser presents his neck to be licked, sometimes kneeling [S Toronto Zoo].
- **About rearing.** None of the mouflon sources I found describe rearing on the hind legs before the clash. All the rearing descriptions I found concern the North American bighorn. **Animate the documented sequence instead:** back off a few metres → short rush with lowered head → head-on clash → recoil and stand.

**Vocal repertoire** [S ANCGG, DE Wikipedia, waldwissen]:
- Alarm hiss or whistle through the nose.
- Bleats as contact calls.
- Lambs bleat like domestic lambs.
- A ewe's short, low bleat after giving birth.
- The ram's rattling and snarling during the rut.
- Adults do not wail.

**Head carriage summary** [P/E]:
- **Alert:** high, neck near vertical.
- **Walking:** about back level.
- **Grazing:** on the ground.
- **Fight approach:** lowered, horns forward.
- **Rest:** lying on the sternum with legs folded, head up, chewing the cud.

## 9. The 10 details that say "Corsican mouflon" (not a domestic sheep or another wild sheep)

1. **Smooth short hair, no fleece**, in wild chocolate and chestnut tones. A woolly or uniform-coloured coat suggests a domestic sheep or a hybrid [S ANCGG, Larousse].
2. **The ram's heavy, closely-coiled horns:** ridged, rounded-triangular in cross-section, about 3/4–7/8 of a turn in profile, hugging the head with the tips toward the neck or throat. They are **never divergent at the tips** (homonymous) and do not curl up to the eye as in many continental rams [S OFB, PNA].
3. **The white saddle on the upper flank** behind the shoulder, in winter rams. It is on the side, not on top of the back [S PNA, INPN].
4. **Black forequarters and black throat ruff** (jabot) against **chestnut hindquarters** in winter [S PNA, INPN, OFB].
5. **A black band along the lower flank** separating the brown side from the **white belly** [S PNA, OFB].
6. **White "stockings"** below the knees and hocks, with a dark front to the forearm and a narrow dark line down the front of the forelegs [S PNA, ANCGG, OFB].
7. **White muzzle and mask plus a white eye ring** on a grey-brown face, and a **convex (Roman) nose** in the adult ram [S PNA, ANCGG, IT Wikipedia].
8. **A very short dark tail** (5–8 cm) sitting in a **white rump patch**. The pale rump is more marked than in urial or argali [S PNA, waldwissen, Larousse].
9. **Small size and compact, deep body on slim legs.** Only about 75 cm at the withers, among the smallest wild sheep [S INPN, PNA, waldwissen]. The bighorn is much bigger: 65–125 cm at the withers and 60–140 kg for males [S Larousse]. Argali are far larger, and urials taller with a long black-and-white bib [E, general knowledge not checked in these sources].
10. **Short pointed ears** set under the horn base, and **lateral eyes** with an amber iris and a **horizontal pupil** [S PNA, DocCheck; P].

**Common mistakes to avoid:**
- A domestic-sheep fleece.
- A long tail.
- Horns that flare out at the tip, or horns that are thin and smooth.
- A saddle drawn across the spine.
- Forgetting the flank band.
- An all-white face (only old Bavella-type rams get very pale faces).
- Long or drooping ears.
- Round or vertical pupils.
- A 90-cm "deer-like" size.
- Too much orange (a sunset effect, not the local colour).
