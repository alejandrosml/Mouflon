# Valais Blacknose: visual specification for a side-view illustration

**Breed names:** Valais Blacknose (EN) · Walliser Schwarznasenschaf / "Schwarznase" (DE) · Nez noir du Valais (FR) · Schwarznasenschaf (IT herd book). Older names: Visp sheep, Visperschaf.
**Compiled:** 2026-11-03, from breed standards and agricultural sources, plus close inspection of about 15 reference photos.

**How to read this:** Source tags in square brackets point to section 12. **[Photos]** means something I saw in the reference photos listed in section 12. **Estimate** means a number I worked out from photos and general sheep anatomy; no standard gives it.

The original Swiss standard (sn-verband.ch/rassenstandard.php) blocks automated access. These sources reproduce it:
- **[VBS-UK]** and **[VBSANA]** are English translations of it. Both say so explicitly.
- **[LfL]**, **[AT]** and **[Walser]** quote its German wording, for example "nicht unterbunden" and "kurz schwarz gestiefelt".

---

## 0. At a glance

- **Subject:** an adult **ewe**, 2 to 4 years old, in full fleece (about 5 to 6 months of growth, locks 10 to 15 cm long). She stands square, faces right, with her head up and alert.
- **The look in one sentence:** a big, shaggy, cream-white sheep with these features:
  - a **black "mask"**: muzzle plus eye area, reaching up to mid-forehead, set inside a white woolly hood
  - a **white curly topknot**
  - **black ears** sticking out sideways under pale **spiral horns**
  - **black spots on the front of the knees** and **on the back of the hocks**
  - **black "boots"** on all four feet
  - **no other black anywhere**

**Core palette** (details in section 4.9)

| Element | Base | Light | Shadow |
|---|---|---|---|
| White wool, clean or show fleece | `#EEE7D8` | `#F8F4EA` | `#CFC2A6`, deep `#9C8C70` |
| White wool, alp or field fleece (unwashed) | `#E4D8C0` | `#F1EBDD` | `#BFAE8E`, beige tips `#D2BE98` |
| Black markings (wool) | `#161414` | curl sheen `#3B3633` | `#0B0A0A` |
| Nose leather, lips | `#24262A` | `#55585E` (wet sheen) | `#121316` |
| Horn | `#CDB693` | ridges and tip `#E6D9C0` | `#9A7D5C`, streaks `#4F3D2E` |

## 1. Identity, and which animal to draw

**Origin.**
- The breed comes from the Upper Valais (Oberwallis), Switzerland, and the neighbouring Bernese Oberland [WP-EN].
- It has been recorded since about the 15th century [VBS-UK][WP-EN][OSU].
- It earlier "resembled the likes of a Scottish Blackface" [VBS-UK].
- The breed society, the Oberwalliser Schwarznasen-Schafzuchtverband, was founded in 1948 [WP-DE]. The breed was officially recognised in 1962 [WP-EN][OSU][WP-DE].
- It is a dual-purpose mountain breed for wool and meat. It spends the summer on alpine pastures (June/July to September) and is housed in winter [VBS-UK][Zermatt].
- About 13,700 animals are in the Valais flock book [VBS-UK]. FAO DAD-IS (2023) gives 9,380 herd-book ewes [WP-EN].
- It was exported to the UK in 2014 and has since spread to the USA (semen-based upgrading), New Zealand and elsewhere [OSU][RBNZ].

**Choice: an adult ewe.** Both sexes are horned, but the ewe is the better subject:

1. **She is what people picture.** Ewes and lambs make up almost all of any flock. The famous "world's cutest sheep" photos show the teddy-bear face, the topknot and the black boots, and they come mostly from ewe flocks on the Zermatt and Gornergrat alps.
2. **She carries the complete marking set of the standard**, including the **ewe-only black tail spot**. Rams must *not* have it [VBS-UK][VBSANA][LfL][AT][SE].
3. **She reads better in side view.** Ewe horns are thinner and smaller [VBSANA]. The black ear, the mask, the topknot and the white wool between them all stay visible. A mature ram's horn coil covers the ear and the whole rear of the head in profile. At small sizes the ram reads as "a horned ram" rather than "a Valais Blacknose".
4. **Her temperament fits a friendly character:** calm, social, gentle and inquisitive (section 8).

Draw her **correctly marked**, the kind of animal that would score maximum points (6/6/6 for animals over 2 years in Swiss grading [VBS-grading]). Choose a ram only if you want a "majestic emblem". Section 10 lists everything that changes for a ram.

## 2. Size and proportions

### 2.1 Official figures

| | Ewe | Ram | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Height at withers (adult) | **72 to 78 cm** (LfL: 70 to 78) | **75 to 83 cm** (NZ and IT: 75 to 85; LfL: 80 to 85) | [VBS-UK][VBSANA][SE][IT][NZVBSS][LfL][WP-EN][Walser] |
| Live weight (adult) | **70 to 90 kg** (WP-DE, Alpsee, SE intro: 70 to 80) | **80 to 125 kg** (NZ and IT: 80 to 120; LfL, WP-EN, Walser: 80 to 130; WP-DE, Alpsee: 80 to 100) | same, plus [WP-DE][Alpsee] |
| Body length | **not given by any standard** | not given | see 2.2 |
| Maturity | slow-maturing; fully grown at about 3 years [SE][VBS-UK] | | |

Note on [VBSANA]: it gives ewes 23.3 to 30.7 in. The lower figure is evidently a typo for 28.3 in (72 cm).

### 2.2 Drawing proportions (ewe, H = height at withers, about 75 cm)

These are **estimates** from side-on photos of *shorn* animals (the real body) and of full-fleece animals.

**The body under the wool (shorn ewe and shorn ram photos):**
- **Leg length, ground to elbow, is about 0.5 H**, the same as **body depth (withers to brisket), about 0.5 H**.
- Body length, point of shoulder to pin bone: about **1.15 to 1.2 H**, so **about 85 to 90 cm** for a ewe and roughly 90 to 100 cm for a ram.
- **Head length**, poll to muzzle tip: about **0.33 to 0.37 H** (about 25 to 28 cm).
- **Neck** [VBS-UK][VBSANA][NZVBSS] is "short, straight, well-muscled". It is strong and of moderate length when shorn.
- Knee (carpus) about 0.25 H above the ground. Hock about 0.3 H above the ground.
- **Natural tail** about 0.45 to 0.5 H long. Its tip reaches to just above the hock (see 2.3).

**In full fleece (the iconic look):**
- Wool adds **10 to 15 cm** on the topline, the sides, the chest and the breech. The belly wool hangs 10 to 15 cm below the brisket as a ragged fringe, the "skirt".
- The woolly body reads as a **deep, rounded box or barrel**. Its length is about 1.4 to 1.6 times its own height. The topline is nearly straight [VBS-UK: "long, straight top line"] and the rump is rounded.
- **Visible leg below the belly fringe** is about **0.3 to 0.4 H** in 10 cm fleece. In long late-summer fleece it drops to about **0.25 H** [Photos: Zermatt animals].
  - So the legs look like only **a quarter to a third of the total silhouette height**.
  - The visible leg is only **about 0.5 to 0.7 times the depth of the woolly body**.
  - The legs are woolly too, so they read as short, thick columns.
- **The head looks small:** the woolly body is about **3.5 to 4 head lengths** long, counting the topknot.
- **The neck nearly disappears.** The head seems to come straight out of a woolly bib on the chest.
- **Alert head carriage:** the top of the topknot sits about half to one head-height above the woolly topline, and the muzzle is roughly at topline height. When grazing, the head goes down to the ground.

### 2.3 Tail: long, wool-covered, usually natural

- **[SE]:** "The ideal length reaches down to the black markings on the hock." Docking is *not allowed* in Sweden except for medical reasons.
- **[VBSANA]:** "Full horns, full wool and tails are important". At shows: "No docking of tails shorter than three fingers above hocks."
- **Switzerland [BLV]:** docking is allowed only up to day 7 of life, and "Der Schwanzstummel muss mindestens 15 Zentimeter lang sein" (the stump must be at least 15 cm long). Docking is banned from 1 February 2040.
- **UK [VBS-pts]:** points are deducted if the "tail [is] not docked in accordance with animal welfare guidelines", which implies docking is common there. In UK show photos the tail sticks out of the breech wool and looks shorter than hock length on some animals [Photos: Royal Highland Show].
- **Shorn animals [Photos]** show a thin, natural tail hanging close to the breech with its tip at hock level.

**Draw it:** a natural, white, wool-covered tail. In fleece it is a thick woolly "rope", about 8 to 12 cm wide, hanging against the back of the thighs. Its lock tassel ends just above the black hock patch. In profile it mostly merges into the rear fleece. Show it with a vertical shadow groove between the tail and the buttock, plus the tip.

## 3. Fleece

**Type.**
- [LfL]: "Die mit Grannenhaaren durchsetzte Wolle ist grob, lang und gelockt im D-E Charakter". Coarse, long, curly wool, mixed with guard hairs.
- [VBSANA]: "Wool is double coated."
- [SE] and [WP-DE]: a fine undercoat plus a long outer coat "with curls and waves at the tips", with some coarser guard hairs.
- [IT]: "di tipo aperto o semiaperto": an **open or semi-open** fleece of separate locks, not a dense, blocky fleece.
- The whole body is wooled "von der Stirn bis zu den Klauen" (from forehead to claws) [AT].

**Fibre diameter.**
- 28 to 38 µm: finest in lambs, coarsest in adult rams [VBS-UK][VBSANA][SE].
- About 38 µm [WP-EN, citing Mason].
- Adult target 36 to 40 µm [NZSBA].
- Lambs are soft; the fleece coarsens with age [VBS-UK].

**Growth and staple length (sources disagree):**
- about **10 cm per 6 months** [VBS-UK][NZVBSS][SE]
- **4 to 6 in per about 6 months** [VBSANA]
- **7 to 8 cm after 180 days** [LfL][IT]
- "about 30 cm a year" [NZSBA]
- 100 mm or more [WP-EN]

**Shearing and presentation:**
- Sheep are shorn **twice a year**, in spring (February/March) and in autumn (end of September). Head and legs are shorn too [VBS-UK][FWI][VBS-tips].
- Show preparation:
  - US show ideal: "wool should be 5-6″" (13 to 15 cm) [VBSANA].
  - UK graders want at least 10 cm [VBS-tips].
  - "Fleece is too long" can cost points [VBS-pts].
- **No trimming or shaping.**
  - "Trimming or shaping wool with scissors" costs points [VBS-pts].
  - [VBSANA] treats fitting or blocking as a disqualification.
  - [VBS-tips]: "Avoid trimming wool around the face after shearing."
- So the iconic, photographed animal has **10 to 15 cm of natural, untrimmed locks everywhere**, including the face and legs.

**What the locks look like** [Photos: fleece close-up, Zermatt animals, show animals]:
- **Long, pointed, separate locks** about 1 to 2 cm wide at the base.
  - They are **wavy along their length**: roughly 2 to 4 soft S-bends per 10 cm.
  - They end in **loose curls or ringlet tips**.
  - They are lustrous [SE: "lustrous"; "relatively lanolin-poor"].
- **In long fleece** (late summer, 12 to 20 cm), the locks **hang vertically down the flanks** like shaggy ringlets.
  - A faint parting may show along the spine.
  - A wool "bib" hangs off the chest and neck, and a fringe hangs off the belly.
  - The outline is ragged and scalloped, never smooth.
- **In short or young fleece** (lambs, or about 2 to 4 months after shearing), the coat is a dense mat of tight, crinkly curls, like astrakhan (teddy-bear look) [Photos: Camborne show lamb].
- A few coarse guard hairs stick out, giving a fine fly-away halo when backlit.
- **Fault note:** [SE] lists "spiralformad ull" (spiral-shaped wool) as a wool fault. [AT] lists "Zwirn" next to kemp. [VBS-UK] and [VBSANA] translate the same Swiss fault list as "kemp and/or tender fibres", "excessive guard hairs" and "felting".
  - These are fibre-quality faults and the translations disagree. They do **not** ban curly locks: the German standard asks for "gelockt" (curly) wool.
  - Draw **wavy locks with curly tips**, not uniform tight corkscrew ringlets like a Wensleydale.

**Colour.**
- "Uniformly white and balanced over the whole body" [VBS-UK][VBSANA][NZVBSS][LfL].
- "No brown or grayish color" (of the markings) [VBSANA].
- In real life:
  - Washed show sheep are **creamy white**.
  - Alp sheep are **cream to light beige**, with yellowish-beige tips from lanolin and dust.
  - Winter or dirty fleece turns **sandy beige** (about `#CDB894`) [Photos].

**Head wool, the topknot.**
- "Wool on head is natural and long" [VBS-UK][VBSANA][NZVBSS].
- A large, curly, white **forelock or topknot** covers the forehead above the mask and the whole poll, and sits between the horn bases. Its curls overhang the top edge of the black mask and often fall over the eyes.
- **White wool also covers the cheeks behind the mask, the jowls, the ear bases and the throat.** The black face looks set inside a white woolly hood.
- Breeders today favour hairier faces and legs [WP-DE].

**Leg wool.**
- "Wool on legs is thick and full" [VBS-UK][VBSANA][NZVBSS]. "Leg wool should be similar to the length with body" [VBSANA].
- Legs are wool-covered right down to the black socks. The knee and hock spots are patches of **black wool** within the white leg wool.

**Black fibres.**
- A few black hairs are tolerated on the neck: in females only [VBS-UK][VBSANA]; in animals over 18 months [NZVBSS]; or in all animals except rams over 18 months [SE].
- For a clean drawing, leave them out.

## 4. Black markings: exact positions and edges

The marking rules are the same for both sexes, except the tail spot (ewes only) and the scrotum (rams).

### 4.1 Face mask (nose plus eyes, joined)

**What the sources say:**
- [VBS-UK]/[VBSANA]: "Black nose to the center of the head." "Black encompassing the eye area connecting with the black of the nose, no gap is permitted."
- [LfL]: "Der Kopf ist an der Nase bis zur Mitte und nicht unterbunden bis zu den Augen schwarz umrandet." The head is black from the nose to the middle, unbroken up to and around the eyes.
- [IT]: "il naso e metà della testa sono neri e la colorazione nera si prolunga senza interruzione sino agli occhi" (the nose and half the head are black, unbroken to the eyes).
- [SE]: up to half the head is black, continuing round the eyes with no gap.

**Drawing it in profile, facing right:**
- **Extent:** black covers the front **half to about two-thirds of the side of the head**:
  - the whole muzzle (lips, chin, nostrils)
  - the nasal bridge up to **mid-forehead**, roughly level with or slightly above the eye
  - a **complete black ring around the eye**
  - the cheek below and in front of the eye, down to the lower jaw line
- **Upper edge:** a rounded arch at mid-forehead. Its line is broken by white topknot curls hanging over it.
- **Rear edge:** from mid-forehead it curves down **2 to 4 cm behind the eye** to the angle of the jaw. **Between the mask and the ear base there is white cheek and poll wool** (see 4.2).
- **Lower edge:** follows the lower jaw. The throat is white, though a few long black curls may hang from the jaw [Photos].
- **Texture:** the black is **curly black wool** (short to medium, astrakhan-like), not sleek hair. Only the nose leather, nostrils and lips are bare dark skin.
- **Faults (do not draw):**
  - a white bridge between the nose black and the eye black
  - grey or brown tint [VBS-pts: "Grey or brownish discolouration of markings on head and legs"]
  - patchy, "intermediate" colour defects [VBS-pts]

### 4.2 Ears

- [VBS-UK]/[VBSANA]/[NZVBSS]: "Black ears – black should meet the white of the head." [LfL]: "Die Ohren sind bis an den Kopf schwarz." [SE]: black up to where the head starts; "the black should not continue onto the head".
- **Draw:** the **whole ear black, both surfaces, right to the base.** Short black hair or wool covers it. The base is surrounded by **white** head wool, with a clean edge.
- **Faults:** a white ear base, or black spreading onto the head around the ears [VBS-pts].

### 4.3 Front knees (carpus)

- [VBS-UK]/[VBSANA]: "Black spots on knees and hocks (spots can be non-symmetrical)."
- [VBSANA]: "Knee spots need a white line of separation between knee spots and ankle area. Presence is important, not the size."
- [LfL]: "schwarzen Flecken … an den Karpalgelenken". [AT]: "an … den vorderen Knien". [SE]: "on the **front** of the front knees".
- **Draw:**
  - An **oval black patch on the front face of each knee**, at about 0.25 H above the ground. In profile it shows as a black oval on the leading edge of the foreleg.
  - Size estimate: about the height of the joint, roughly 6 to 10 cm.
  - White wool above it (forearm) and below it (cannon). **The white band between the knee spot and the sock must be visible.**
  - Left and right may differ in size.
  - In long fleece the belly fringe may partly overhang the knee spot.

### 4.4 Hocks (hind legs)

- [LfL]: "schwarzen Flecken an den Sprunggelenkhöckern" (black spots on the hock "points", the calcaneal tuber). [SE]: "black at the **back** of the hocks".
- **Draw:** an **oval or round black patch on the rear edge of each hind leg at the hock**, at about 0.3 H. White above and below it, and a **white cannon between the hock spot and the sock**.

### 4.5 Feet, the "black socks" or "boots"

- [VBS-UK]/[VBSANA]: "Black hooves and ankle area ('black socks')." [LfL]: "Die Beine sind kurz schwarz gestiefelt" (short black boots). [SE]: black around the fetlocks "like black shoes", with a clear white band between pasterns, knees and hocks.
- **Draw:**
  - Hoof, coronet, pastern and fetlock all black, on all four legs.
  - The **top edge is roughly level, just above the fetlock**. Estimate: 8 to 12 cm above the ground, about 0.12 to 0.15 H.
  - White leg wool hangs slightly over the edge, so it is soft, but it should read as a clean band.
- **Fault:** "Black sock has white indent or black travels up in to the white of the leg" [VBS-pts].

### 4.6 Tail spot (ewes only)

- [VBS-UK]: "Females are to have a black tail spot. There must be a finger-width gap of white on the tail." "Males must not have a tail spot."
- [VBSANA]: a "black spot under the tail … at least a pencil-width gap of white on the visual side of the tail".
- [SE]: a black spot "around the vulva/anus, a finger's width of white … at the base of the tail".
- [LfL], literal text: "Das Fehlen eines Fleckes oder schwarze Flecken am Schwanzansatz ist für männliche Tiere ein Ausschlussgrund, bei weiblichen Tieren wird der fehlende Fleck geduldet. Erwünscht ist ein nicht allzu großer Schwanzfleck." My reading: in rams, a missing (leg) spot or black at the tail base disqualifies; in ewes a missing spot is tolerated; and a not-too-large tail spot is desired.
- [AT]: "Weibliche Tiere haben einen schwarzen Afterfleck, welcher bei männlichen Tieren nicht geduldet wird."
- **Draw (profile):** it is mostly **hidden under the tail**. At most, a thin black crescent peeks out between the tail and the buttock at tail-root height. The **outer, visible face of the tail stays white**. From behind, it shows as black on both sides of a white tail.

### 4.7 Belly and scrotum

- **Rams:** the scrotum may be black, and black may reach the lower belly, "but must not spread further upwards towards the tail or beyond the naval area" [VBS-UK]. [VBSANA]: "Belly can be black, just not above the 'Skirt or Belly Line'."
- [SE]: black around the navel is allowed in both sexes.
- [IT]: "ampie e diffuse macchie scure sull'addome" (large, widespread dark spots on the belly) exclude an animal from the herd book.
- None of this is visible in a standing side view of a ewe. Keep her belly white.

### 4.8 Everything else is white

- "No black on the body" [FWI].
- "Black body spots on the main body area" is a serious fault [VBS-UK][VBSANA].
- No black wool at the horn base [VBSANA].
- "Spitti" are reverse-marked, mostly black lambs that occur occasionally (about 1 in 25 to 50 [SE]). They are **not** the breed type and should not be drawn unless intended.

## 5. Head

- **Overall shape:** "Well proportioned, compact head", "Roman nose, wide mouth, broad forehead" [VBS-UK][VBSANA][NZVBSS]. [SE]: "relatively short, broad head". [LfL]: "Der Kopf wird charakterisiert durch die Ramsnase, das breite Maul, die mittellangen und getragenen Ohren sowie die Hörner."
- **Profile:** a **Roman (convex) nose**, called "Ramsnase" in German, "nez busqué" in French [WP-FR], and "leggermente montonino" (slightly ram-nosed) in Italian [IT].
  - Ewe: a gentle, continuous convex arch from forehead to nostrils.
  - Ram: a stronger arch.
  - "Lack of roman nose" costs points [VBS-pts].
  - The nose is curved enough that halters slide down it, so halters are banned at US shows [VBSANA].
- **Muzzle:** broad, deep and blunt, with a wide mouth. General sheep anatomy:
  - Nostrils are comma-shaped slits angled up and outward on a bare, dark nose pad.
  - The upper lip is split (philtrum).
  - All of this sits inside the black.
- **Eyes:**
  - Placed laterally, high on the side of the head, roughly halfway to three-fifths of the way from muzzle tip to poll (estimate). Always **inside the black mask**.
  - Often half hidden by black curls, so the eye reads as a dark glint in a black field [Photos].
  - **The iris colour is not specified by any standard.** The close-up photo shows a **light brown, amber-hazel** iris that looks grey-brown in shade.
  - The **pupil is horizontal and rectangular/oblong**, as in all sheep [Photos].
  - Eyelids are black. Entropion or ectropion disqualifies breeding stock [SE].
- **Ears:**
  - Size: "mittellange" (medium-long) [LfL], "medium sized" [SE], "di grandezza media" [IT]. **Estimate:** about 0.4 to 0.5 times the head length.
  - Carriage:
    - "getragene Ohren" (carried, not hanging) [LfL]
    - "portate obliquamente verso il basso" (carried obliquely downward) [IT]
    - [Photos] the ears **stick out sideways from just below and behind the horn base**, roughly horizontal to 10 to 30° below horizontal. Not erect and not flat-hanging. Lambs hold them straight out.
  - Shape: a leaf or lancet shape, widest in the middle, with a rounded-pointed tip.
  - **In profile, facing right:** the near ear projects backward and outward, toward the viewer, from under the horn's arc. It is partly foreshortened, and its black base sits in a collar of white wool behind the mask. The far ear is mostly hidden, or its tip shows beyond the forehead.
- **Forehead wool:** the white topknot (section 3). Natural and long, never trimmed. In profile it makes a big rounded or fluffy dome from mid-forehead back over the poll, rising above the top of the skull.
- **Rams only:** "Pronounced, regal head shape in rams. Mature rams often develop protuberance behind horn base" [VBS-UK][VBSANA]. [SE]: a strong pad on the neck behind the horn base when fully grown.

## 6. Horns

### 6.1 What the sources say

**Both sexes:**
- [LfL]: "Beide Geschlechter tragen große weiße und schraubenförmige Hörner, die von den Backen gut abgesetzt sind und waagrecht abwachsen." Large, white, screw-shaped horns, well clear of the cheeks, growing outward horizontally.
- [Walser]: "schraubenförmig-spiralig und seitlich vom Kopf abstehend" (screw-spiral, standing out sideways from the head).
- [IT]: "corna spiralate a vite verso l'esterno" (screw-spiral, outward).
- [VBS-UK]/[VBSANA]: "Spiral outward from head, horizontally away from cheeks (You should always be able to fit a flat hand comfortably between the horn and cheek). Black stripes in the horns are acceptable." [VBSANA] adds: "just no black wool at base."
- [SE]: spiral-shaped, growing out from the head, **"med en plattare sida"** (with one flatter side), with at least 1 cm of clearance from the head. Horns are "light", with occasional black streaks tolerated. They should be carried "like an ornament".

**Ewes:**
- [VBSANA]: "Female horns are thinner, spiral slightly down, and then curl out to the side."
- [VBS-pts]/[NZVBSS]: "Spiral, well set off from the cheeks; horizontal or curved."
- [RBNZ]: "smaller and point outward". [NZSBA]: "lighter and generally point outwards". [Alpsee]: "horizontal, spiral".

**Rams:**
- [VBSANA]: "Male horns are thicker, spiral forward, down, and then curl out to the side."
- [VBS-pts]: "curved in large spirals, well set off from the cheeks."
- [NZSBA]: "heavier and spiral downward".

**Number of turns:** no standard gives one. **From photos:**
- ewe: about **half to three-quarters of a turn**; some older ewes curl to about one turn around the ear [Photos: shorn ewe]
- mature ram: about **1 to 1.5 turns** [Photos: show ram, museum ram]

### 6.2 Side view, animal facing right (you see its left horn)

**Ewe:**
1. **Base:** on top of the skull at the poll, above and slightly behind the eye. It is half buried in the topknot. The two bases are a few cm apart, with topknot wool between them.
2. **Path:**
   - The horn rises slightly, then sweeps **backward** (to the left in the picture) and **outward**, toward the viewer.
   - It bends **down** behind the ear base.
   - It finishes with a slight forward and outward turn. The tip ends at about ear level, pointing out to the side and a little forward or down.
3. **In pure profile** it looks like a **crescent or "C" opening forward** (to the right), turning counter-clockwise as you see it. Its outer diameter is about **a third to half of the head length**. The black ear sticks out just under or through its lower arc.
   - Many ewe horns point mostly *sideways* ("waagrecht"). In strict profile these foreshorten into a short ring or stub on top of the head [Photos: Zermatt profiles].
   - To make the horn read, turn the head a few degrees toward the viewer, or show more of the curl.
4. **Clearance:** the horn must stand clear of the cheek and must never touch the face.
5. **Far horn:** hidden behind the head. At most, its upper arc shows above the topknot.

**Ram** (other sex, for reference):
- Thick bases on the top of the skull, separated only by the topknot.
- **Path:** up and back (left), then down behind the ear, then **forward under the ear**, then **out and up**. The tip points forward and outward at about eye-to-mouth-corner level, a hand's width outside the cheek.
- In profile it is a **big, nearly circular coil** about as tall as the head. It runs from above the poll down to about the level of the corner of the mouth, and from just behind the ear base forward to about the eye.
- The ear comes out from under or inside the coil, pointing back and down.
- **Seen from the front:** the horns flare out like an open corkscrew. **Tip-to-tip span is about 2.5 to 3 times the head width** (wool included), an estimate from two mature-ram photos.

### 6.3 Cross-section, surface and colour

- **Cross-section:** one flatter face [SE].
  - Rams: broad and roughly **triangular**, with a flat front and outer face and fairly sharp edges, tapering strongly to a blunt, smoother tip [Photos].
  - Ewes: slimmer, **flattened oval**.
  - **Estimate for base thickness:** ram horn base about a quarter to a third of the head height; ewe horn base about an eighth to a sixth.
- **Surface:** **close-set transverse growth ridges** (corrugations) run along the whole horn. They are coarse and deep on rams, finer on ewes. The last few cm of the tip are smoother and paler [Photos].
- **Colour:**
  - "weiß" (white) [LfL]; "ljusa" (light) [SE].
  - In photos: **ivory or cream to light caramel-tan**. The ridge crests are lighter, the grooves and underside darker.
  - **Darker grey-brown streaks** along the horn are allowed [VBS-UK][VBSANA][SE].
  - Older rams' horns often look honey-brown overall.
  - Use the horn row in section 4.9.

## 7. Legs and hooves

- **Conformation** [VBS-UK][VBSANA][NZVBSS]:
  - "Strong, sure footed"; "solid bone structure"; "Joints should be straight and sound".
  - Forelegs have a "broad stance and [are] not knock-kneed". Hind legs are "not cow-hocked or bowlegged", with "slight hock angulation".
  - "Short, strong pasterns (45-degree angle)".
  - [VBSANA]: "Legs are to be more under the sheep than to the side."
- **Proportion:** the legs are anatomically medium, with ground-to-elbow about equal to body depth (section 2.2). **Visually short** in fleece.
- **Wool:** thick and full down to the socks, about as long as the body wool [VBSANA].
  - The legs read as **thick, slightly tapering cream columns with ragged lock fringes**.
  - Down each leg the colour goes: white upper leg, **black knee spot** (front legs) or **black hock spot** (hind legs), **white cannon band**, **black boot**.
- **Hooves:** **black** [VBS-UK][VBSANA][WP-FR]. Cloven, compact, with a dark slate hoof wall and a faint grey sheen. "Solid hoof deportment".
  - They are small relative to the woolly legs. Estimate: each claw about 5 to 6 cm long on a ewe.
  - The black of the hoof runs straight into the black sock wool.

## 8. Temperament and behaviour (for animation)

**Character:**
- "Quiet docile animals that are friendly and inquisitive" [NZSBA]. "Very social, calm" and suited as companion animals [SE]. "Placid" [FWI]. "Peaceful" [Alpsee].
- "kinderfreundlich" (good with children), with temperament, sure-footed and adaptable [AT].
- "standorttreu" (stays in its home range), frugal, hardy and weather-resistant [LfL][Walser][WP-DE].
- A skilled climber on steep, rocky high pastures [WP-DE][Alpsee].
- On hot days they **seek shade** because of the thick fleece [Zermatt].
- They are flock animals and stay close together [Photos].

**Gait** (from the standard, [VBS-UK][VBSANA][NZVBSS]): "Spacious, sure footed and wide legged; doesn't sway or waver; even and stable movement; mobile with ease." The movement should look **steady and planted**, not wobbly.

**Animation notes** (general sheep behaviour, not specific to this breed):
- **Walk:** four-beat, lateral sequence (left hind, left fore, right hind, right fore). The head nods slightly with each foreleg step. The short visible legs make the steps look short and quick under a big, steady body.
- **Trot:** diagonal two-beat. **Gallop or bound** only when startled. Lambs gambol and leap on all four legs.
- **Secondary motion:** the heavy fleece lags and bounces after each step. Long locks and the belly fringe swing. The topknot bobs. The tail sways a little. Ears flick independently.
- **Idle poses:**
  - grazing, with the head down to the ground and forelegs straight
  - alert, with the head high and ears out sideways
  - lying chest-down, legs folded under, chewing cud, which makes a very rounded "wool loaf" silhouette
  - scratching the face against a leg or a post
- **Voice:** no source describes a breed-specific voice. Use a normal sheep bleat ("baa", "mäh"):
  - ewes: medium-low and soft, with a low rumbling murmur to their lambs
  - lambs: high-pitched
  - rams: deeper

## 9. The 10 details that make it unmistakably a Valais Blacknose

1. **Black mask, not a black head.** Black covers the muzzle and wraps around both eyes with no white gap, and stops around **mid-forehead**. The rest of the head is a **white woolly hood**.
2. **Big white curly topknot** on the forehead and poll, overhanging the mask.
3. **Ears black right to the base**, sticking out **sideways under the horns**, with a ring of white wool where they meet the head.
4. **Pale spiral horns in both sexes.** They are ivory to light tan, ridged, and grow outward clear of the cheeks.
5. **Black knee spots on the front of the forelegs**, separated from the boots by a **white band**.
6. **Black hock spots on the back of the hind legs**, also separated from the boots by white.
7. **Short black "boots"** on all four feet (hoof, pastern, fetlock), with a fairly clean top edge. Hooves are black.
8. **Long, coarse, wavy-curly white locks over the whole body, including the face and legs.** The fleece is shaggy and untrimmed, and the leg wool reaches down to the boots.
9. **Roman nose** and a broad, blunt muzzle.
10. **Large, deep, barrel-shaped woolly body that hides the legs**, with **no black anywhere on the body**. The tail is long and woolly, and in ewes there is a hidden black spot under it.

**Not to be confused with:**
- **Suffolk:** **no horns**. **Head and legs entirely black and free of wool** (bare, short black hair). Long black ears. Short, dense, smooth white fleece. No topknot, no knee or hock spots.
- **Black-headed mutton sheep (Schwarzköpfiges Fleischschaf, the German black-headed meat breed, also kept in Switzerland)** [LfL-SF]:
  - "Der mittelbreite Kopf ist hornlos und ebenfalls schwarz": **no horns**, the **whole head black** (forehead only partly woolly).
  - Ears strong and carried sideways.
  - Legs **dark brown to black from knee or hock down and "möglichst unbewollt"** (as wool-free as possible).
  - **Dense, uniform** white crossbred fleece. No separate knee or hock spots.
- **Scottish Blackface:** horned in both sexes, but with a **bare, black or black-and-white mottled face** and bare legs. No woolly face, no topknot, no white hood framing a black mask.

**Do not draw:**
- black spots on the body
- brownish or greyish markings
- a white ear base, or black spreading onto the head
- a white gap between the nose black and the eye black
- missing knee or hock spots
- white notches in the boots
- clipped, blocked or sculpted wool
- horns touching the cheeks
- a tail spot on a ram

## 10. Differences for a ram (the other sex)

| Feature | Ewe (chosen) | Ram |
|---|---|---|
| Height and weight | 72 to 78 cm; 70 to 90 kg | 75 to 83 (up to 85) cm; 80 to 125 kg (up to 130 in some sources) |
| Head | compact, gentle Roman nose | "Pronounced, regal head shape", stronger Roman nose. Mature rams have a **protuberance or pad behind the horn base** and a thick, crested neck |
| Horns | thinner; about half to three-quarters of a turn; mostly sideways or slightly down ("horizontal or curved") | **thick, heavy, 1 to 1.5 turns**: "spiral forward, down, and then curl out to the side". Large coil around the ear; tips flare outward; hand-width clear of the cheek |
| Ears | clearly visible under the horn | partly hidden inside or under the horn coil |
| Tail spot | **required** (hidden under the tail) | **must not have one** (disqualifying) |
| Scrotum and belly | n/a | scrotum may be black; black may reach the lower belly, not past the navel or belly line |
| Black neck hairs | a few tolerated | a fault in young rams (up to 18 months); disqualifying in rams over 18 months in [SE] |
| Wool | 28 to 38 µm range | the coarsest wool (adult males) |
| Missing knee or hock spot | point deduction | serious fault, grading score of 1 [VBS-UK][VBSANA][LfL] |

**Lambs**, if needed:
- Born with the full black pattern: mask, ears, knee and hock spots, boots.
- Ewe lambs already show the tail spot, so lambs can be sexed at birth [NZSBA].
- Short, tight, crinkly curls; horn buds only; long natural tail; legs look longer relative to the body [Photos].

## 11. Where sources disagree

| Topic | Disagreement |
|---|---|
| Ram weight | 80 to 100 [WP-DE][Alpsee]; 80 to 120 [NZVBSS][IT]; 80 to 125 [VBS-UK][SE][WP-FR]; 80 to 130 kg [LfL][WP-EN][Walser]. US: 175 to 275 lb (about 79 to 125 kg) |
| Ewe weight | 70 to 80 kg [WP-DE][Alpsee] versus 70 to 90 kg (all the standards) |
| Withers | rams 75 to 83 [VBS-UK][SE] versus 75 to 85 [NZVBSS][IT] versus 80 to 85 cm [LfL]; ewes 70 to 78 [LfL] versus 72 to 78 cm (others) |
| Staple growth | 7 to 8 cm per 180 days [LfL][IT]; 10 cm per 6 months [VBS-UK][NZVBSS][SE]; 4 to 6 in per 6 months [VBSANA]; about 30 cm per year [NZSBA] |
| Fibre diameter | 28 to 38 µm [VBS-UK][VBSANA][SE]; about 38 µm [WP-EN]; adult goal 36 to 40 µm [NZSBA] |
| White gap on the tail (ewe tail spot) | finger-width [VBS-UK][NZVBSS][SE] versus pencil-width [VBSANA] |
| Horn clearance from cheek | a flat hand [VBS-UK][VBSANA] versus at least 1 cm [SE] |
| Horn colour | "weiß" (white) [LfL] versus light, with black stripes acceptable [VBS-UK][VBSANA][SE] |
| Ewe horn direction | "horizontal or curved" [VBS-pts]; "spiral slightly down, then out" [VBSANA]; "point outward" [RBNZ][NZSBA]. Photos show both types |
| Ram horn direction | "forward, down, then out" [VBSANA]; "spiral downward" [NZSBA][RBNZ]; [Alpsee]'s "curve inward" looks like a loose translation |
| Tail docking | not allowed (Sweden) [SE]; allowed up to day 7 with at least 15 cm stump, banned from 2040 (Switzerland) [BLV]; not shorter than 3 fingers above the hocks [VBSANA]; docking "in accordance with welfare guidelines" expected [VBS-pts] |
| Black neck hairs | females only [VBS-UK][VBSANA]; animals over 18 months [NZVBSS]; all except rams over 18 months [SE] |
| Wool faults | "Zwirn" [AT] is translated as "spiral-shaped wool" [SE], "tender fibres" [VBS-UK][VBSANA], and "tangles" [VBS-pts] |
| Body length | **no source gives it**; the 85 to 90 cm ewe figure is my estimate from photos |
